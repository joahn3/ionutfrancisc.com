# LinkedIn Content Strategy — Senior Cybersecurity / Privacy / GRC

**Status:** Active planning  
**Cadence:** 1 post / week for 6 weeks  
**Profile:** https://www.linkedin.com/in/ionut-iulian-francisc

## Objective

Build a small, consistent and credible LinkedIn publishing rhythm that reinforces positioning for **Senior Cybersecurity / Privacy / GRC** roles without becoming overly promotional or "marketing loud".

The intended signal is seniority through practical governance judgment, risk ownership, operational maturity, audit readiness and clear accountability.

## Six-week calendar

### Week 1 — Security exceptions

**Post:** What an exception log looks like when it helps the team instead of blocking it.  
**Purpose:** Show governance thinking, risk judgment and ownership.

### Week 2 — Handover with clear risk

**Post:** “What does a good handover look like when the control is not perfect at release?”  
**Purpose:** Differentiate through operational maturity and explicit risk transfer.

### Week 3 — Security / Privacy by Design

**Post:** Three simple questions to ask at the beginning of any project.  
**Constraint:** Keep it process-oriented; do not rely on named client or project examples.  
**Purpose:** Demonstrate a practical rather than theoretical approach.

### Week 4 — Audit readiness

**Post:** What to check before an audit to reduce friction.  
**Purpose:** Demonstrate applied GRC and assurance experience.

### Week 5 — Decision records & accountability

**Post:** What separates “formally approved” from “actually controlled.”  
**Purpose:** Signal risk/compliance leadership and decision discipline.

### Week 6 — Incident governance

**Post:** Minimum information that should exist after an incident: ownership, timeline, remediation and action owners.  
**Purpose:** Reinforce a senior incident-governance profile.

## Content rules

1. Prefer short **text-only** posts: approximately 4–7 lines plus bullets where useful.
2. Keep hashtags minimal: **3–4 maximum**.
3. Use at most **one anonymous mini-case**, focused on process rather than a named client or project.
4. Every 2–3 weeks, include a short professional reflection or lesson learned.
5. Only **one of the six posts** should end with an open question for natural engagement.
6. Avoid aggressive self-promotion, detailed Sezamo/IBM references, and service-listing posts.
7. Prefer practical observations, decision patterns, ownership, evidence and remediation over generic cybersecurity advice.

## Drafting workflow

1. Draft the next 2–3 posts.
2. Review for factual accuracy, confidentiality and senior-level tone.
3. Remove unnecessary company/client identifiers and unsupported claims.
4. Approve the final text before publication.
5. Publish one post per week.
6. Record the published URL and any useful qualitative feedback before changing the content strategy.

## Scheduling decision

Do **not** lock dates yet. Choose one of these only after the first 2–3 drafts are approved:

- **Fixed calendar:** explicit publication dates.
- **Rolling weekly cadence:** one approved post every seven days.

The cadence matters more than volume; consistency and signal quality take priority over posting frequency.
